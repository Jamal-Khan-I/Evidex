1. ADMISSIBILITY OF ELECTRONIC EVIDENCE UNDER SECTION 63 OF BHARATIYA SAKSHYA ADHINIYAM (BSA), 2023:
   The prosecution has presented digital exhibits (Exhibit-A through Exhibit-E) secured and registered on the Evidex Ethereum Sepolia Smart Contract Ledger. The defense challenged the chain of custody asserting potential tampering during police custody and laboratory transit.

   FINDINGS OF THE COURT ON ELECTRONIC ADMISSIBILITY:
   - The Court examined the cryptographic SHA-256 integrity hash verification and the immutable timestamped chain of custody logged directly on the decentralized Ethereum blockchain.
   - The forensic examination report (CFSL/CYBER/2026/REP-0419) confirmed that the cryptographic hash of each physical disk image and audio bitstream at point of collection precisely matches the hash evaluated during CFSL laboratory examination.
   - Under Section 63(2) & 63(4) of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (read with Section 79A of IT Act 2000), the digital evidence exhibits satisfy all statutory requirements of integrity, mathematical immutability, and chain of custody preservation. The exhibits are hereby formally ADMITTED into evidence without exception.

2. VERDICT & PRONOUNCEMENT:
   Upon meticulous scrutiny of forensic acoustic spectrograms proving voice deepfakes, GSM SIM-Box automated IVR logs, and bank-to-crypto laundering trails, the Court finds the prosecution's charges proven beyond reasonable doubt.

   The Court hereby pronounces:
   - Accused No. 1 (Rajesh Varma): GUILTY OF CONVICTION under:
     * Section 318(4) Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023 (Cheating)
     * Section 336(3) BNS, 2023 (Forgery of Court & Law Enforcement Seals)
     * Section 66D Information Technology Act, 2000 (Cheating by Personation)
   - Accused No. 2 (Sunil Kumar): GUILTY OF CONVICTION under:
     * Section 318(4) read with Section 61(2) BNS, 2023 (Criminal Conspiracy & Mule Layering)
